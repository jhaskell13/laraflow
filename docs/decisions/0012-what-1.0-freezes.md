# 0012 — What 1.0 freezes, and what it deliberately does not

**Status:** Accepted (Milestone 12). Extends [0006](0006-a-small-final-public-surface.md).

## Context

Until now **Public** has meant *intended to become stable* ([public-api.md](../public-api.md)): a promise about the future, with breaking changes allowed in any minor as long as the changelog carried them. 1.0 is where that promise comes due.

At 0.7.0 the surface is 149 classes carrying no stability marker and 40 files marked `@experimental`. The experimental set is not a fringe — it is every console command, the dashboard, every trigger class, the metrics snapshot, the tracing hook, and both bundled connectors. A 1.0 that froze the libraries and left everything an operator actually touches free to move would be the opposite of what a 1.0 is for: the console and the dashboard are what a deploy pipeline and a runbook are written against.

Three questions had to be answered together: what Public means after 1.0, which experimental surfaces are promoted, and how the freeze is *enforced* rather than promised.

## Decision

**Public means covered by semantic versioning.** From 1.0, a breaking change to anything [public-api.md](../public-api.md) marks **Public** requires a major release, preceded by the deprecation path in [compatibility.md](../compatibility.md#how-a-deprecation-works). Experimental still means what it has always meant, and Internal still carries no promise at all.

**The freeze is enforced by a test, not by care.** An API signature baseline — every Public class's public method signatures, constants, enum cases, and property types, committed to the repository — with a test that fails on any change to it. Changing the baseline is then a deliberate, reviewable diff that has to argue for itself, exactly as `SchemaFreezeTest` makes a schema change argue for itself. A promise a machine does not hold is a promise this project has learned not to make.

**Promoted to Public at 1.0:**

| Surface | Why |
|---|---|
| Console command **names**, and the `--json` envelope (`command`, `ok`, `error`) | Deploy pipelines, cron entries, and health checks are written against these. Freezing them is most of the operational value of 1.0. |
| Per-command `--json` **fields** | Public but **additive-only**: new keys may appear in a minor, existing ones may not change meaning or type. Already the documented rule ([inspection-and-observability.md](../inspection-and-observability.md#machine-readable-output)); 1.0 makes it binding. |
| `Dashboard` and the `Triggers` surface | Both have shipped two minors, are proven over real HTTP in CI, and are named in [SECURITY.md](../../SECURITY.md)'s scope. A surface whose security is in scope is not experimental. |
| `Snapshots`, `MetricsSnapshot`, `Tracer`, `NullTracer` | One minor old, but small, contract-tested, and the whole of the observability story. A 1.0 whose metrics and tracing hooks "may change or be removed without notice" has no observability story. |
| `GithubConnector`, `SlackConnector`, and their error mappers | [public-api.md](../public-api.md) said they were "kept experimental until the first release". This is that release. |

**Deliberately still Experimental at 1.0: `ExecutionStore`**, and with it the store half of `ExecutionRepository`. This is not an oversight to be tidied away — it is [0006](0006-a-small-final-public-surface.md) holding. Every milestone so far has widened the store, and its shape has been proved against exactly one driver. A store method graduates to Public when a second driver has implemented it unchanged. The split is precisely what lets 1.0 promise a stable *read* API without claiming the write protocol is finished, and the promise is worth more for being narrow.

**Internal at 1.0: `Laraflow\Demo\*`.** They exist for one command. Experimental implies someone might reasonably build on them; Internal says plainly that nobody should.

**The bundled connectors stay in this repository.** [0011](0011-one-optional-package.md) sent metrics bridges out because running an application's monitoring is not a Laravel package's job. That reasoning does not transfer: calling an external service *is* the job — it is half of what Laraflow is for — and a developer who needs a connection inside a workflow should find one in the package rather than a recipe for writing one. The bundled two also earn their place as proof: `ProjectContractsTest` fails if either ever reaches past the public contracts, which is what makes "no Laraflow file needs editing to add a connector" a fact rather than a claim.

## Rejected

- **Freezing everything, the store included.** It would make the 1.0 promise bigger and falser at once, and would be broken by the first alternative persistence driver — the exact thing the interface exists to make possible.
- **Keeping the console experimental at 1.0.** It is the most-scripted surface in the package. Leaving it unfrozen would mean a 1.0 that cannot safely appear in anyone's crontab.
- **Splitting the connectors into their own packages,** by analogy with 0011. The analogy is false, and the cost is real: a second repository, a second release cadence, and a contract test with nothing in-repo left to hold.
- **Freezing the per-command `--json` documents field for field.** Fields have been added in every milestone and adding one breaks no reader that reads by key. Additive-only is the honest constraint.

## Consequences

The 1.0 surface is large but bounded, and a machine holds it. Anything not yet right has to be made right *before* the tag, because afterwards it waits for 2.0 — which is why the freeze is a line-by-line review of every Public class, not a declaration. `ExecutionStore` remains the one place a minor may still move, and [compatibility.md](../compatibility.md#alternative-persistence-drivers) says so where a driver author will read it.
